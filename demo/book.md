# A short book about books

## Pages, not a strip

Scrolling has no sense of place; pages do. A page holds a fixed amount, a
number says where you are, and a turn marks where one stretch of reading
ends and the next begins.

A long text, a pull request, a book in EPUB: each is laid out as facing
pages across two panes, the text running on from the left page to the
right, and turned rather than scrolled.

### The measure

Rows are held to a readable length, with margins around them, however wide
the pane. A reader who wants them longer, or shorter, says so once for each
book, and the book remembers.

### The turn

A turn is drawn: the free edge of the sheet crosses the right page and then
the left, the next pages appearing behind it.

## Ribbons and notes

A bookmark is a ribbon, hanging from the top of its page into the margin by
the gutter, in one of six colours. A note goes on a page, on a row, or on a
stretch of text chosen with the mouse, and shows as a footnote at the foot
of its page.

### Markers

A highlighter laid over chosen text keeps its colour, and opens when it is
clicked: to change its colour, write its note, or take it away.

### Finding the way back

The bookmarks and notes are listed together, in reading order, each one
step away from its page.

## Books bound on the right

Japanese books and manga run the other way: the right page first, turns
sweeping from left to right. A book can be turned round, and keeps the way
it was set.

## Asking

A question about the pages in front of you goes to the agent beside the
book, with the pages themselves. It answers in its own pane, and leaves a
short answer in the book, where the question was asked.

## Finding

A search finds as it is typed, steps from find to find, and lists them all
with the words around each, so that any one of them is a step away.
